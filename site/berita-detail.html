REKAM at the Global Coral Reef Stage of ICRS 2026: Showcasing Indonesia's Ocean Accounts

Coral reefs are among the world's most valuable marine ecosystems. Beyond supporting extraordinary biodiversity, they provide essential ecosystem services that sustain fisheries, tourism, coastal protection, and the livelihoods of millions of people. Safeguarding these benefits requires management approaches that connect ecological conditions with their social and economic values through integrated and evidence-based information.

This commitment was reflected in Rekam Nusantara Foundation's participation in the 16th International Coral Reef Symposium (ICRS), held in Auckland, New Zealand, from 19 to 24 July 2026. The symposium brought together more than 2,100 participants from 93 countries, making it one of the world's leading scientific forums dedicated to coral reef research and conservation.

Representing REKAM, our Ocean Accounts Program Manager Annisya Rosdiana and National Coordinator Lailatul Rokhmah presented Indonesia's research on coral reef valuation and Ocean Accounts to the international scientific community.

During the session on reef tourism and biodiversity resilience, Lailatul Rokhmah presented research on the economic value of coral reef ecosystem services in the Gili Matra Marine Protected Area. The study demonstrated that coral reef ecosystem services continue to retain and even increase their economic value despite natural changes in reef conditions. These findings reinforce the importance of sustained conservation efforts to preserve both the ecological integrity of coral reefs and the economic benefits they provide to coastal communities.

In a separate session on long-term coral reef monitoring, Annisya Rosdiana introduced Indonesia's Ocean Accounts Dashboard. The platform demonstrates how spatial, ecological, and economic data can be integrated into a single framework to support marine protected area monitoring and strengthen evidence-based marine policy and decision making.

Together, these presentations showcased REKAM's ongoing efforts to share Indonesia's progress in Ocean Accounts with the global community. By integrating natural capital accounting with scientific evidence and policy needs, Ocean Accounts offers a practical approach to supporting more adaptive, transparent, and sustainable coral reef management.

Indonesia's Ocean Accounts initiative is the result of strong collaboration among the Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries, the Ministry of National Development Planning (Bappenas), the Ministry of Finance, Statistics Indonesia (BPS), the Geospatial Information Agency (BIG), and Rekam Nusantara Foundation, with support from the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP), the Government of the United Kingdom, and the Government of Norway through Norad. This partnership demonstrates that sustainable ocean governance is built through collaboration, innovation, and the effective use of integrated data to support better decisions for the future of marine ecosystems.
