Overview

Indonesia holds some of the largest tropical forests left on earth, and no complete account of what lives in them.

A few species carry the attention: the tiger, the orangutan, the rafflesia. Behind them are thousands that keep the forest running and have never been studied, many of them named by the people living alongside them long before any scientist arrived. The pressure does not wait for the science. Forest is cleared for what it returns this season, and what goes with it was never written down.

Forest works on both gaps at once. We survey the species nobody has funded, train young researchers to keep surveying after us, and build the records with the communities whose knowledge of these forests is older than ours. Camera traps, acoustic monitoring, and field survey, run by people who will still be here in twenty years.

A forest that is documented can be argued for. One that is not simply disappears quietly, and the argument is over before it starts.
